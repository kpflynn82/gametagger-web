# Action-Based Genres

## Action RPG

**Definition**: Games that combine real-time combat with role-playing elements including character progression, skill trees, equipment systems, and stat-based damage calculations. Combat requires active player input (timing, positioning, combos) rather than menu-based commands.

**Includes**:
- Diablo IV
- Elden Ring
- Path of Exile
- Monster Hunter World
- Grim Dawn

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Final Fantasy XVI** → While action-heavy, primarily categorized as JRPG due to linear narrative structure and Japanese development philosophy
- **Hades** → Roguelike (procedural runs with permadeath are the defining feature, not persistent progression)
- **Destiny 2** → Looter Shooter (shooting mechanics are primary; RPG elements are secondary)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 1-3 hours (longer than pure action games)
- Retention drivers: Loot drops, character builds, endgame content
- Monetization: Premium with DLC expansions; some F2P with cosmetics
- Player investment: High (100+ hours typical for completionists)

---

## Action Adventure

**Definition**: Games that blend combat, exploration, and puzzle-solving in a cohesive world. Typically feature a mix of melee combat, traversal challenges, and environmental puzzles without deep RPG stat systems.

**Includes**:
- The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom
- God of War (2018)
- Uncharted 4
- Tomb Raider (2013 reboot)
- Star Wars Jedi: Survivor

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Elden Ring** → Action RPG (deep stat systems, build variety, and loot are central)
- **Assassin's Creed Valhalla** → Action RPG (level-gated content, gear scores, skill trees dominate)
- **Metroid Dread** → Metroidvania (ability-gated exploration is the core loop)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 1-2 hours
- Completion rate: Higher than average (linear narrative drives completion)
- Monetization: Premium, single-purchase model
- Replay value: Low to moderate

---

## First-Person Shooter (FPS)

**Definition**: Games where the primary gameplay involves shooting from a first-person perspective. The player's view represents the character's eyes, with weapons visible on screen.

**Includes**:
- Call of Duty: Modern Warfare III
- Halo Infinite
- DOOM Eternal
- Counter-Strike 2
- Overwatch 2

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Cyberpunk 2077** → Action RPG (shooting is one of many systems; RPG progression is central)
- **Fortnite** → Battle Royale (the last-standing competitive format defines it, not the shooting)
- **Destiny 2** → Looter Shooter (loot progression is the primary engagement driver)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 20-45 minutes per match (multiplayer); 1-2 hours (campaign)
- Engagement pattern: High-frequency, short sessions
- Monetization: Battle passes, cosmetics, seasonal content
- Competitive population: Often split between casual and ranked

---

## Third-Person Shooter (TPS)

**Definition**: Shooting-focused games where the camera is positioned behind and above the player character, showing their full body. Emphasizes cover mechanics, spatial awareness, and character visibility.

**Includes**:
- Gears of War 5
- The Division 2
- Max Payne 3
- Resident Evil 4 Remake
- Outriders

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Grand Theft Auto V** → Sandbox (shooting is one activity among many; open-world freedom defines it)
- **Mass Effect** → Action RPG (RPG systems, dialogue, and narrative are equally important as combat)
- **Control** → Action Adventure (supernatural abilities and exploration are as central as shooting)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 30-90 minutes
- Cover-based combat leads to more methodical pacing
- Often features co-op modes with higher retention
- Campaign completion rates typically higher than FPS due to narrative focus

---

## Bullet Hell

**Definition**: Shooting games characterized by overwhelming numbers of projectiles on screen that the player must navigate through precise movements. Patterns are typically memorizable but require split-second reactions.

**Includes**:
- Touhou Project series
- Ikaruga
- Enter the Gungeon
- Cuphead (boss fights)
- Vampire Survivors

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Hades** → Roguelike (while it has projectile dodging, melee combat and progression are primary)
- **DOOM Eternal** → FPS (fast-paced but not pattern-based projectile dodging)
- **Returnal** → Roguelike/TPS (bullet hell elements but not the defining characteristic)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 15-45 minutes (high intensity limits longer sessions)
- Retry rate: Very high (designed for repeated attempts)
- Skill ceiling: Extremely high
- Monetization: Typically premium, indie pricing

---

## Beat 'em Up

**Definition**: Side-scrolling or arena-based games focused on hand-to-hand combat against waves of enemies. Emphasizes combos, crowd control, and often co-op play. Distinct from fighting games by featuring multiple simultaneous enemies rather than 1v1 duels.

**Includes**:
- Streets of Rage 4
- Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles: Shredder's Revenge
- Devil May Cry 5
- Bayonetta 3
- Sifu

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Mortal Kombat 11** → 2D Fighting (1v1 structured matches, not wave-based combat)
- **God of War Ragnarok** → Action Adventure (combat is part of broader exploration/puzzle structure)
- **Sekiro** → Souls-like (precise 1v1 dueling and death penalties define it)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 30-90 minutes
- Co-op engagement: Significantly higher retention in multiplayer
- Completion rate: High (typically 6-12 hour campaigns)
- Replay value: Moderate (character unlocks, difficulty modes)

# Shooter Sub-Genres

## Looter Shooter

**Definition**: Shooting games where the primary engagement loop is acquiring increasingly powerful gear through repeated combat encounters. Gear is **persistent** between sessions, and progression is measured in equipment quality rather than player skill alone.

**Key Distinguishing Features**:
- Persistent inventory across all play sessions
- Gear rarity tiers (common, rare, legendary, etc.)
- Power level or gear score systems
- PvE-focused grinding loops

**Includes**:
- Destiny 2
- Borderlands 3
- The Division 2
- Warframe
- Outriders

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Fortnite** → Battle Royale (no persistent gear; every match starts fresh)
- **Escape from Tarkov** → Extraction Shooter (gear is at risk each raid; can be permanently lost)
- **Call of Duty: Warzone** → Battle Royale (loot is session-based only)
- **Diablo IV** → Action RPG (loot exists but melee/magic combat, not shooting, is primary)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 1-3 hours (grinding requires investment)
- Daily active users: High due to daily/weekly reward systems
- Monetization: F2P or premium with season passes and cosmetics
- Player investment: Very high (hundreds of hours for endgame)
- Retention pattern: Spikes at content drops, valleys between seasons

---

## Extraction Shooter

**Definition**: Tactical shooters where players enter instanced raids with gear that is **at risk of permanent loss**. Success means extracting with loot; failure means losing brought equipment. The risk/reward tension is the core experience.

**Key Distinguishing Features**:
- Gear brought into raids can be permanently lost on death
- PvPvE format (players compete while fighting AI)
- Extraction points create tension and strategic decisions
- High stakes create intense moment-to-moment gameplay

**Includes**:
- Escape from Tarkov
- Hunt: Showdown
- The Finals
- Dark and Darker
- Marauders

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Destiny 2** → Looter Shooter (gear is never at risk; only time is lost)
- **PUBG** → Battle Royale (no gear persistence between matches at all)
- **The Division 2** → Looter Shooter (Dark Zone has extraction but gear loss isn't the core loop)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 20-45 minutes per raid
- Anxiety/intensity: Very high (gear loss creates real stakes)
- Player skill variance: Extreme (veterans dominate new players)
- Monetization: Premium purchase, often with optional cosmetics
- Retention: Polarizing (players either commit heavily or bounce quickly)

# RPG Variants

## JRPG

**Definition**: Role-playing games developed with Japanese design philosophy, typically featuring linear narratives, predefined characters, turn-based or semi-turn-based combat, and anime-influenced aesthetics. Party management and character relationships are central.

**Includes**:
- Final Fantasy XVI
- Persona 5 Royal
- Dragon Quest XI
- Xenoblade Chronicles 3
- Tales of Arise

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Elden Ring** → Action RPG/Souls-like (open-world design, Western-influenced difficulty philosophy)
- **Genshin Impact** → Action RPG (gacha mechanics and open-world exploration define it more than JRPG structure)
- **Pokémon Scarlet/Violet** → Turn-Based RPG (creature collection is the defining feature)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 2-4 hours (narrative-driven extended sessions)
- Completion time: 60-100+ hours
- Player investment: Very high emotional attachment to characters
- Monetization: Premium with potential DLC story content
- Demographic: Strong overlap with anime/manga audiences

---

## Turn-Based RPG

**Definition**: RPGs where combat occurs in discrete turns, with players and enemies alternating actions. Strategy comes from action selection, party composition, and resource management rather than reaction time.

**Includes**:
- Pokémon Scarlet/Violet
- Baldur's Gate 3
- Divinity: Original Sin 2
- Octopath Traveler II
- Sea of Stars

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Final Fantasy XVI** → JRPG/Action RPG (real-time combat system)
- **Fire Emblem: Engage** → Tactical RPG (grid-based positioning is the primary mechanic)
- **Persona 5** → JRPG (while turn-based, the social sim elements and JRPG structure are more defining)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 1-3 hours
- Pacing: Methodical; appeals to strategic thinkers
- Mobile potential: High (turn-based works well for interrupted play)
- Completion rate: Moderate (length can cause dropoff)

---

## Tactical RPG

**Definition**: RPGs played on grid-based battlefields where unit positioning, terrain, and turn order are critical strategic elements. Combines RPG character progression with chess-like tactical combat.

**Includes**:
- Fire Emblem: Engage
- Triangle Strategy
- XCOM 2
- Disgaea 7
- Tactics Ogre: Reborn

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Baldur's Gate 3** → Turn-Based RPG (while tactical, the D&D ruleset and narrative choices are more central than grid tactics)
- **Civilization VI** → 4X Strategy (no individual character progression; empire-level strategy)
- **Into the Breach** → Puzzle/Strategy (puzzle-like optimization rather than RPG progression)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 1-2 hours (natural break points between battles)
- Decision density: Very high per session
- Permadeath variants: Create strong emotional attachment and restart potential
- Audience: Overlaps heavily with chess and strategy board game players

---

## MMORPG

**Definition**: Persistent online worlds where thousands of players simultaneously inhabit the same server, featuring character progression, guilds, raids, and long-term social structures.

**Includes**:
- World of Warcraft
- Final Fantasy XIV
- Guild Wars 2
- The Elder Scrolls Online
- Lost Ark

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Destiny 2** → Looter Shooter (instanced content, not persistent open worlds with thousands)
- **Genshin Impact** → Action RPG (primarily single-player with limited co-op, not massively multiplayer)
- **Diablo IV** → Action RPG (shared world spaces but not MMORPG social structures)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 2-6 hours (raid content can run 3+ hours)
- Player investment: Extreme (thousands of hours over years)
- Monetization: Subscription or F2P with premium options
- Social dependency: High (guild obligations drive retention)
- Churn risk: Losing social connections causes cascade departures

---

## Roguelike

**Definition**: Games featuring procedurally generated levels, permadeath (or significant death penalties), and run-based progression where each attempt is a fresh start. Meta-progression may persist between runs.

**Includes**:
- Hades II
- Dead Cells
- Slay the Spire
- The Binding of Isaac
- Returnal

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Elden Ring** → Souls-like/Action RPG (death is punishing but not a full restart; world is handcrafted)
- **Diablo IV** → Action RPG (death has minimal consequences; progression is persistent)
- **Enter the Gungeon** → Bullet Hell (while it has roguelike elements, the bullet patterns define gameplay more)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 30-90 minutes per run
- Replay value: Extremely high
- Completion definition: Varies (first win vs. full completion vs. achievements)
- Skill progression: Player skill matters more than character power
- Streaming appeal: High (each run is unique content)

# Platformers

## 2D Platformer

**Definition**: Games focused on running, jumping, and navigating 2D side-scrolling environments. Precision movement and timing are the core skills.

**Includes**:
- Celeste
- Hollow Knight
- Super Mario Bros. Wonder
- Shovel Knight
- Ori and the Will of the Wisps

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Super Mario Odyssey** → 3D Platformer (3D movement and exploration)
- **Metroid Dread** → Metroidvania (ability-gating and backtracking define it beyond platforming)
- **Cuphead** → Bullet Hell (boss pattern memorization is primary)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 30-60 minutes
- Difficulty variance: Ranges from casual to extremely hardcore
- Completion rate: Varies wildly by difficulty
- Speedrun community: Very active for precision platformers

---

## 3D Platformer

**Definition**: Games centered on navigating 3D environments through jumping, climbing, and traversal mechanics. Collecting items and exploring levels are common secondary objectives.

**Includes**:
- Super Mario Odyssey
- Ratchet & Clank: Rift Apart
- Psychonauts 2
- A Hat in Time
- Astro Bot

**Does NOT Include**:
- **The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom** → Action Adventure (combat and puzzles are equally important as traversal)
- **Uncharted 4** → Action Adventure (platforming serves the narrative rather than being the focus)
- **Mirror's Edge** → Action Adventure (first-person parkour with different design philosophy)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 1-2 hours
- Family friendly: Often rated E or E10+
- Collectible completion: Drives extended playtime
- Nostalgia factor: Strong for players who grew up with N64/PS1 era

---

## Metroidvania

**Definition**: 2D exploration games where progress is gated by abilities acquired throughout the game. Players must backtrack to previously inaccessible areas as they gain new powers. Interconnected world maps and ability-based progression are defining features.

**Includes**:
- Hollow Knight
- Metroid Dread
- Ori and the Blind Forest
- Blasphemous
- Axiom Verge 2

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Celeste** → 2D Platformer (linear progression, no ability gating)
- **Dead Cells** → Roguelike (procedural generation and permadeath override metroidvania elements)
- **Dark Souls** → Souls-like (while interconnected, 3D and combat-focused rather than ability-gated exploration)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 1-2 hours
- Map completion: Key engagement metric
- Backtracking: Expected and designed into the experience
- Player skill: Moderate requirement (exploration skills + combat)

# Strategy Genres

## Real-Time Strategy (RTS)

**Definition**: Strategy games where all players act simultaneously, commanding armies, gathering resources, and making tactical decisions in real-time. Multitasking and actions-per-minute (APM) are key skills.

**Includes**:
- StarCraft II
- Age of Empires IV
- Company of Heroes 3
- Warcraft III: Reforged
- Command & Conquer Remastered

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Civilization VI** → 4X Strategy (turn-based, not real-time)
- **Total War: Warhammer III** → Grand Strategy (hybrid real-time battles but turn-based campaign)
- **League of Legends** → MOBA (controls single hero, not army)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 20-60 minutes per match
- Skill ceiling: Very high (professional esports scene)
- Learning curve: Steep (macro and micro management required)
- Audience trend: Declining but dedicated core fanbase

---

## Turn-Based Strategy

**Definition**: Strategy games where players take discrete turns to move units, manage resources, and execute plans. Thoughtful planning replaces reaction speed.

**Includes**:
- Civilization VI
- Into the Breach
- Advance Wars 1+2: Re-Boot Camp
- Wargroove
- Phoenix Point

**Does NOT Include**:
- **XCOM 2** → Tactical RPG (individual soldier progression and narrative make it RPG-adjacent)
- **Fire Emblem** → Tactical RPG (character relationships and RPG elements are central)
- **StarCraft II** → RTS (real-time, not turn-based)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: Variable (turns allow natural pause points)
- Mobile compatibility: High
- Multiplayer engagement: Often lower than single-player
- Think time: Players appreciate being able to plan without time pressure

---

## Tower Defense

**Definition**: Games where players strategically place defensive structures to prevent waves of enemies from reaching a goal. Resource management and placement optimization are key.

**Includes**:
- Bloons TD 6
- Kingdom Rush
- Plants vs. Zombies
- Arknights
- Dungeon Defenders II

**Does NOT Include**:
- **They Are Billions** → RTS/Survival (active unit control, not just tower placement)
- **Orcs Must Die 3** → Action/Tower Defense hybrid (direct player combat is equally important)
- **Clash of Clans** → Management Simulation (base building and attacking, not wave defense)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 15-30 minutes per map
- Mobile dominance: Very strong on mobile platforms
- Monetization: Often F2P with IAP for towers/heroes
- Replay value: High (optimization and difficulty modes)

---

## 4X Strategy

**Definition**: Grand-scale strategy games featuring four core gameplay pillars: eXplore, eXpand, eXploit, and eXterminate. Players build civilizations or empires over long time spans.

**Includes**:
- Civilization VI
- Stellaris
- Endless Legend
- Humankind
- Old World

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Europa Universalis IV** → Grand Strategy (historical simulation focus rather than 4X structure)
- **Age of Empires IV** → RTS (real-time combat, not 4X pacing)
- **Tropico 6** → Management Simulation (single island focus, not empire expansion)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 2-8 hours (famous for "one more turn" syndrome)
- Completion rate: Low (campaigns can exceed 20+ hours)
- Save/load frequency: Very high
- Multiplayer: Often async or very long sessions

---

## Grand Strategy

**Definition**: Deep political and military simulations typically set in historical periods. Focus on diplomacy, internal politics, economic management, and warfare at a national or empire level.

**Includes**:
- Crusader Kings III
- Europa Universalis IV
- Hearts of Iron IV
- Victoria 3
- Total War: Warhammer III

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Civilization VI** → 4X Strategy (more abstracted, less historical detail)
- **XCOM 2** → Tactical RPG (squad-level focus, not nation-level)
- **Risk of Rain 2** → Roguelike (despite "strategy" elements)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 3-10+ hours
- Learning curve: Extremely steep
- Tutorial completion: Often low (complexity overwhelming)
- Mod community: Very active and extends game lifespan significantly
- Niche audience: Dedicated but smaller than mainstream strategy

---

## Auto Battler

**Definition**: Strategy games where players draft and position units that then fight automatically. Strategy comes from team composition, positioning, and economy management between rounds rather than direct control during combat.

**Includes**:
- Teamfight Tactics
- Dota Underlords
- Super Auto Pets
- Storybook Brawl
- Hearthstone Battlegrounds

**Does NOT Include**:
- **League of Legends** → MOBA (direct real-time control of champion)
- **Fire Emblem Heroes** → Tactical RPG (direct unit control during combat)
- **Clash Royale** → Card Game/Strategy (real-time card deployment, not auto-battle)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 20-40 minutes per match
- Match frequency: High (quick games encourage repeated play)
- Mobile performance: Very strong
- Monetization: Cosmetics, battle passes, and character unlocks
- Skill expression: Draft decisions and positioning over mechanical skill

---

## MOBA (Multiplayer Online Battle Arena)

**Definition**: Team-based competitive games where players control a single hero with unique abilities, competing to destroy the enemy team's base. Combines RTS elements (lanes, minions, structures) with RPG progression within matches.

**Includes**:
- League of Legends
- Dota 2
- Smite
- Heroes of the Storm
- Pokémon Unite

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Overwatch 2** → FPS/Hero Shooter (no lanes, minions, or base destruction)
- **Battlerite** → Arena Brawler (no lanes or traditional MOBA structure)
- **Teamfight Tactics** → Auto Battler (no direct hero control)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 25-50 minutes per match
- Player investment: Extreme (learning all heroes takes hundreds of hours)
- Competitive scene: Major esports with million-dollar prizes
- Toxicity concern: Historically high due to team dependency
- Retention: Very high for engaged players; steep dropoff for casuals

# Simulation Genres

## Life Simulation

**Definition**: Games simulating daily life activities, relationships, and personal development. Focus on managing a character's needs, relationships, and living situation.

**Includes**:
- The Sims 4
- Animal Crossing: New Horizons
- Tomodachi Life
- Story of Seasons series
- My Time at Sandrock

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Stardew Valley** → Farm Simulation (farming mechanics are primary)
- **The Sims Medieval** → Management Simulation (quest-based structure changes core loop)
- **Second Life** → Social Platform (not a game with defined mechanics)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 1-4 hours (easy to lose track of time)
- Demographics: Broader than typical gaming audience
- Monetization: Base game + extensive DLC/expansion packs
- User-generated content: Very important for longevity

---

## Farm Simulation

**Definition**: Games centered on agricultural activities—planting crops, raising animals, and managing a farm economy. Often combined with relationship mechanics and seasonal cycles.

**Includes**:
- Stardew Valley
- Farming Simulator 22
- Harvest Moon/Story of Seasons
- Coral Island
- Sun Haven

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Minecraft** → Sandbox/Open World Survival Craft (farming is optional, not central)
- **Animal Crossing** → Life Simulation (gardening exists but isn't the focus)
- **Slime Rancher** → Management Simulation (creature collection, not traditional farming)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 1-3 hours
- Seasonal engagement: Strong daily/seasonal loops drive retention
- Relaxation focus: Low stress, cozy gameplay
- Completion: Often no "end"—players define their own goals

---

## Management Simulation

**Definition**: Games focused on running businesses, organizations, or systems. Players optimize resources, staff, and operations to achieve growth or efficiency targets.

**Includes**:
- Two Point Hospital
- Planet Coaster
- Football Manager 2024
- RimWorld
- Jurassic World Evolution 2

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Cities: Skylines** → City Builder (urban planning focus rather than business management)
- **Factorio** → Open World Survival Craft (automation puzzle, not business simulation)
- **Stardew Valley** → Farm Simulation (farming mechanics override management aspects)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 2-5 hours (optimization loops are absorbing)
- Replay value: High (different strategies and scenarios)
- Spreadsheet appeal: Attracts analytically-minded players
- Sandbox vs. challenge: Players split between freeform and objective modes

---

## Racing Simulation

**Definition**: Racing games emphasizing realistic physics, car handling, and authentic motorsport representation. Requires understanding of racing lines, tire management, and vehicle setup.

**Includes**:
- Gran Turismo 7
- Forza Motorsport
- Assetto Corsa Competizione
- iRacing
- F1 23

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Mario Kart 8** → Kart Racing (arcade physics, items, accessibility focus)
- **Need for Speed Unbound** → Arcade Racing (stylized, unrealistic handling)
- **Forza Horizon 5** → Arcade Racing (open world, accessible handling model)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 1-3 hours (race length varies)
- Hardware investment: High (wheel, pedals, VR often used)
- Niche but dedicated: Smaller audience with high spending
- Esports: Active sim racing competitive scene
- Realism expectation: Players demand authentic representation

---

## Flight Simulation

**Definition**: Games simulating aircraft operation with varying degrees of realism, from commercial aviation to military combat. Emphasis on authentic flight physics and cockpit procedures.

**Includes**:
- Microsoft Flight Simulator
- DCS World
- X-Plane 12
- Ace Combat 7 (arcade flight sim hybrid)
- IL-2 Sturmovik

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Star Wars: Squadrons** → Space Combat (science fiction, not realistic aviation)
- **Elite Dangerous** → Space Simulation (space ships, not aircraft)
- **War Thunder** → Arcade/Simulation hybrid (often categorized separately)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 1-4 hours (real-time flight durations)
- Hardware investment: Very high (HOTAS, rudder pedals, multiple monitors)
- Learning curve: Steep for study-level sims
- Niche audience: Small but extremely passionate

---

## City Builder

**Definition**: Games focused on designing, building, and managing urban environments. Players balance zoning, infrastructure, services, and citizen happiness.

**Includes**:
- Cities: Skylines II
- SimCity (series)
- Tropico 6
- Frostpunk
- Surviving Mars

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Civilization VI** → 4X Strategy (cities are one element of empire building)
- **Anno 1800** → Management Simulation (production chains and trading are primary)
- **Minecraft** → Sandbox (creative building, not urban management)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 2-5 hours
- Creativity outlet: Players build for aesthetic satisfaction
- Traffic management: Often becomes the core challenge
- Mod support: Critical for longevity (custom assets, mechanics)

# Puzzle Genres

## Puzzle

**Definition**: Games where the primary challenge is solving logic problems, patterns, or spatial challenges. Pure puzzle games focus on cerebral problem-solving without action elements.

**Includes**:
- Tetris Effect
- The Witness
- Baba Is You
- Return of the Obra Dinn
- Portal 2

**Does NOT Include**:
- **The Legend of Zelda** → Action Adventure (puzzles exist but combat and exploration are equally important)
- **Professor Layton** → Narrative Adventure (puzzles serve the story, not the other way around)
- **Celeste** → 2D Platformer (execution challenge, not logical deduction)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 30-90 minutes
- Completion rate: Varies by difficulty
- Hint system usage: Important quality-of-life feature
- Replay value: Low once solved (unless procedural)

---

## Puzzle Platformer

**Definition**: Games combining traversal challenges with puzzle-solving, where figuring out how to progress is as important as executing the solution.

**Includes**:
- Portal 2
- Limbo
- Inside
- Fez
- The Talos Principle

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Celeste** → 2D Platformer (challenges are execution-based, not puzzle-based)
- **Braid** → Puzzle Platformer ✓ (correctly categorized—time manipulation puzzles)
- **Super Meat Boy** → 2D Platformer (pure execution challenge)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 1-2 hours
- Completion rate: Higher than pure platformers (puzzle solutions feel achievable)
- Narrative integration: Often strong (Inside, Limbo)
- Critical acclaim: Genre produces many award winners

---

## Match-3

**Definition**: Puzzle games where players swap adjacent pieces to create lines of three or more matching elements, which then clear from the board.

**Includes**:
- Candy Crush Saga
- Bejeweled
- Puzzle Quest 3
- Marvel Puzzle Quest
- Gardenscapes

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Tetris** → Puzzle (different mechanic—piece placement, not matching)
- **Dr. Mario** → Puzzle (while matching exists, falling piece mechanics differ)
- **Puyo Puyo Tetris** → Puzzle (chain mechanics are distinct from match-3)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 5-20 minutes
- Mobile dominance: Primary platform
- Monetization: F2P with energy systems and power-up purchases
- Daily engagement: Very high for casual players
- Demographics: Broader and older than core gaming audience

## Physics Puzzle

# Multiplayer-Focused Genres

## Battle Royale

**Definition**: Last-player/team-standing multiplayer games where many players drop into a map, scavenge for equipment, and fight until one remains. The match-based format means no gear persists between games.

**Key Distinguishing Features**:
- Large player counts (50-100+)
- Shrinking play area forces encounters
- Scavenged loot is session-only
- Single life per match (typically)

**Includes**:
- Fortnite
- PUBG
- Apex Legends
- Call of Duty: Warzone
- Fall Guys (elimination format)

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Escape from Tarkov** → Extraction Shooter (gear persists outside raids and is at risk)
- **Destiny 2** → Looter Shooter (no battle royale mode; gear fully persists)
- **The Division 2 Dark Zone** → Looter Shooter (extraction exists but not battle royale format)
- **Fortnite: Save the World** → Survival/Action (PvE mode is different genre from Battle Royale)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 15-30 minutes per match
- Daily engagement: Very high (quick matches encourage "one more game")
- Free-to-play dominance: Most successful BR games are F2P
- Monetization: Battle passes, cosmetics, seasonal content
- Streaming appeal: Extremely high (dramatic moments, viewer engagement)
- Player retention: High frequency but individual sessions are short

# Sandbox & Survival Genres

## Sandbox

**Definition**: Open-ended games emphasizing player creativity and freedom with minimal structured objectives. Players create their own goals and experiences.

**Includes**:

- Roblox
- Dreams
- Kerbal Space Program

**Does NOT Include**:

- **Minecraft Survival** → Open World Survival Craft (survival mechanics add structure)
- **Terraria** → Open World Survival Craft (progression systems and bosses provide goals)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: Highly variable (30 minutes to 8+ hours)
- User-generated content: Often the primary content driver
- Completion: No traditional "completion"—players define their own endpoints
- Creative community: Sharing creations extends engagement
- Monetization: Varies (premium, cosmetics, user marketplace)

---

## Survival

**Definition**: Games focused on staying alive against environmental threats, managing hunger/thirst/health, and overcoming hostile conditions. Distinct from Survival Craft by emphasis on survival challenge over building.

**Includes**:
- Subnautica
- The Long Dark
- Green Hell
- Stranded Deep
- Don't Starve

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Minecraft** → Open World Survival Craft (building is equally or more important than survival)
- **Rust** → Open World Survival Craft (base building and PvP dominate)
- **The Forest** → Open World Survival Craft (building is central to progression)
- **Fortnite** → Battle Royale (no persistent survival mechanics)

**Why Fortnite is NOT Survival**:
Fortnite lacks survival's defining features: no hunger/thirst systems, no environmental threats to manage over time, no resource management for staying alive, and no persistence between matches. Each Fortnite match is a fresh competitive start, not an ongoing survival challenge.

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 1-3 hours
- Death penalty: Significant (progress loss)
- Learning curve: Moderate (game teaches through failure)
- Narrative integration: Often environmental storytelling
- Stress level: Moderate to high (resource anxiety)

---

## Open World Survival Craft

**Definition**: Games combining survival mechanics with significant base building and crafting systems in open or procedurally generated worlds. Building and survival are equally important pillars.

**Includes**:
- Minecraft (Survival)
- Valheim
- Terraria
- Rust
- Ark: Survival Evolved

**Does NOT Include**:
- **Subnautica** → Survival (building exists but exploration/survival narrative is primary)
- **The Long Dark** → Survival (no significant building component)
- **No Man's Sky** → Sandbox/Exploration (survival elements are light; exploration dominates)
- **Don't Starve** → Survival (survival challenge outweighs building)

**Expected Metrics**:
- Session length: 2-6 hours (building projects demand time investment)
- Social play: Many feature multiplayer servers
- Content updates: Regular updates keep communities engaged
- Player creativity: Building showcases drive community engagement
- Time investment: Hundreds of hours for engaged players

# Appendix: Genre Selection Guidelines

## When Games Fit Multiple Genres

Many games could be classified under multiple genres. Use these principles:

1. **Primary Engagement Loop**: What does the player spend most of their time doing?
2. **Design Intent**: What was the game designed to deliver?
3. **Player Expectation**: What do players coming to this game expect?
4. **Defining Mechanics**: Which mechanics would change the game most if removed?

## Examples of Multi-Genre Classification

| Game | Could Be | Is Actually | Reason |
|------|----------|-------------|--------|
| Hades | Action RPG | Roguelike | Run-based structure with permadeath is the core experience |
| Destiny 2 | FPS | Looter Shooter | Loot progression is the primary engagement driver |
| Fortnite | Shooter, Survival | Battle Royale | Last-standing competitive format defines the experience |
| Elden Ring | Action Adventure | Action RPG | Deep build variety and stat systems are central |
| Hollow Knight | Souls-like | Metroidvania | Ability-gated exploration is the core progression |
